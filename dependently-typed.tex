\section{Almost reflective dependently typed language:
    MLTT-R}
\label{MLTT-R}

Now we are ready to take on the main challenge: design a
minimal dependently typed reflective language, and
provide internal models of itself to yield universe
polymorphism, external parametricity and do synthetic
mathematics inside it. As a starting point, we take
Martin-L\"of type theory with $\Pi-$,$\Sigma-$,
$\mathrm{Id}-$ types, natural number type and infinite
non-cumulative hierarchy of universes. As discussed in
section \ref{no-go-section}, the best practical solution
to reinterpretation here would be to forbid reductions
under it and reinterpret terms as-is, and constrain
models using $\mathrm{Id}$-types. Still, in comparison
to non-dependently typed case, there are infinitely more
problems to solve if we want to define a truly
reflective system:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item If we na\"{\i}vely adopt an approach to typing
        of models from section \ref{HM-R}, in order to
        specify a model, we would need to specify
        countably many models, one per each universe.
        And even if we did, to which universe does the
        type of models belong?
    \item Now that we have computations in types, we
        have to deal with conversion rule under
        reinterpretation:
        \[
            \dfrac{t:T\quad T\equiv T'}{t:T'}
            \stackrel{?}{\leadsto}
            \dfrac{\llbracket t\rrbracket:\llbracket T
            \rrbracket\quad\llbracket T\rrbracket\equiv
            \llbracket T'\rrbracket}{\llbracket t
            \rrbracket:\llbracket T'\rrbracket}
        \]
        The problem is that, after interpretation,
        $\llbracket T \rrbracket$ is only
        \textit{propositionally} equal to $\llbracket T'
        \rrbracket$.
    \item If we want a truly reflective system, we need
        to know what to do with \textit{reinterpretation
        of reinterpretation}:
        \[
            \lambda x.\llbracket\llbracket x\rrbracket_1
            \rrbracket_2
            \quad\text{and}\quad
            \llbracket\lambda x.\llbracket x\rrbracket_1
            \rrbracket_2
        \]
        In the first case, we perform two subsequent
        interpretations on a yet unknown $x$; in the
        second case, we interpret the process of
        interpretation itself. What should this mean?
\end{enumerate}

To deal with universe sizing issues, consider the
following observation: in a system without level
binders, each term uses only a finite number of levels.
Hence to reinterpret this term, we need to only provide
a finite number of universe models, which can together
fit into some larger universe of a finite level. No
universe polymorphism required! However, we now have to
track the maximal level which appears in a term in our
typing judgements.

To interpret conversion rule, we start by first
pinpointing the locations where conversion rule is
actually used; in order to do this, we \textit{need} to
use bidirectional type checking. At the moment of
conversion, interpretation has enough information to
know both $T$ and $T'$; thus we can translate a chain of
reductions performed during definitional equality check
into a chain of propositional equalities taken from
coherence conditions proven in the definition of a
model. Then, a point of conversion is wrapped in
equality elimination with synthesized witness as a
scrutinee.

In reinterpreting reinterpretation, we actually only
deal with ``double reinterpretation'': cases like
$\lambda x.\llbracket\llbracket x\rrbracket_1
\rrbracket_2$ could be dealt with by \textit{merging}
two models into a new one, a technique reminiscent of
\textit{Collapsing towers of interpreters}
\cite{Amin2017}; it is not required in our system since
the innermost reinterpretation is allowed to compute in
this case. As for interpreting interpretation itself as
in $\llbracket\lambda x.\llbracket x \rrbracket_1
\rrbracket_2$, we explicitly forbid such constructions
by introducing a family of modalities $\Box_\kappa$
meaning ``no interpretation was used in construction of
this term, and all levels appearing in it are not above
$\kappa$''. Terms have to have a boxed type to be
reinterpreted. In the notation for modality and general
typing discipline for it, we largely follow Gratzer et
al \cite{Gratzer2019} with a few important differences
discussed later. We also use $\Box$ to relax a ban on
computations inside reinterpretation by allowing
``splicing'' boxed expressions into other boxed
expressions; inside splices, computation is allowed to
bring terms to the boxed form for further
interpretation.

Grammar rules given in figure \ref{mltt-r-grammar}
reflect discussion above. Note that there is a type of
models $\mathcal{M}$ which is annotated with a sequence
of levels $\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_\kappa$: level $\ell_j$
is a level which types in $\mathcal{U}_j$ would have
after interpretation in such a model.

Typing rules are also a straightforward adaptation of a
general recipe for bidirectional typing of
dependently-typed systems \cite{Thiago2025} and are
given in figures \ref{mltt-r-wf}-\ref{mltt-r-typing};
level placement rules are constructed in such a way
that $\Gamma\vdash_\ell\mathrm{level}(T)=\kappa$ always
implies $\kappa\leqslant\ell$.

Important discrepancy from standard bidirectional
recipe ``constructors are checked, eliminators are
inferred'' is that levels of types are always inferred
since we do not have cumulativity of universes and do
not have a lifting operator, thus we cannot check
levels of $\Sigma$ and $\Pi$. Also note that we provide
levels needed to check $t$ in typal interpretation
$T^t$: this happens because $\mathcal{M}$ would be
definitionally equal to some huge nested $\Sigma$-type,
thus it would be intractable to \textit{match} on it if
it were inferred from $t$ instead.

As already said, reinterpretation is constrained to act
on boxed types; in addition, we require a non-locked
context for reinterpretation. Together these constraints
guarantee that, indeed, boxed terms do not contain
reinterpretation subterms. An important departure from
$\mathrm{MLTT}\text{\faLock}$\cite{Gratzer2019}, aside
from adding a family of modalities, lies in the way
context is locked in $[\_]_\text{\faLock}$ and unlocked
in $[\_]_\text{\faUnlock}$: while locking, we require
the outer context to be unlocked; while unlocking,
instead of erasing all locks, we erase the context up
to the first lock. Effectively, this enforces a
constraint that typing context never contains more than
a single lock. Thus, for example, our $\Box_\ell$ is
not a comonad, but axiom K from modal logic still
holds\footnote{Interestingly enough, a similar
K-modality is first described by Birkedal and
others in ``Modal dependent type theory and dependent
right adjoints'' \cite{Birkedal2020}. Coincidentally,
they refine typing judgements with universe levels,
too! They have a single modality, though.}:
\[
    \dfrac{\cdot\vdash_\ell\mathrm{level}(A,B)=\_}{
        \cdot\vdash_\kappa\lambda f x.[[f
        ]_\text{\faUnlock}[x]_\text{\faUnlock}
        ]_\text{\faLock}:\Box_\ell(A\to B)\to
            \Box_\ell A\to\Box_\ell B
    }(\ell\leqslant\kappa)
\]
In this way, unlocking truly gains the semantics of
``splicing'' in the sense that it can only contain
boxed values from the ``outer world''.

\subsection{Computation rules and models of dependent
type theories}

Note that discussion above was ambient to the choice of
formalism to present models. Now it is finally time to
fix the formalism since we're going to need it to
define where $\mathcal{M}$ computes to.

In line with current state of the art, we use
Categories-with-Families \cite{castellan2020cwf} (or
CwFs for short) as it is currently one of the most
convenient frameworks to specify models of dependent
type theories. Here is a self-contatined definition of
a category with families, given for clear reference in
subsequent internalization:

\begin{definition}[Category]
A category is the following datum\footnote{Of course,
cartesian closed category (\ref{ccc}) is also a
category. We repeat parts of definitions for
self-containment reasons.}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A set of \textit{objects} $\mathrm{Ob}$ and a
    family of \textit{morphisms} $\mathrm{Hom}(S, T)$
    indexed by $S,T\in\mathrm{Ob}$, where
    $f\in\mathrm{Hom}(S,T)$ is denoted by $f:S\to T$. In
    addition, $S$ is called \textit{source} of $f$,
    while $T$ is \textit{target} of $f$, while both $S$
    and $T$ together are referred to as \textit{ends} of
    $f$.
\item For each $A\in\mathrm{Ob}$ there's an
    \textit{identity morphism} $\mathrm{id}_A: A\to A$;
    for each $f:A\to B$ and $g:B\to C$, there's their
    \textit{composition} $g\circ f:A\to C$. If a
    source-target of an identity morphism is clear from
    the context, the subscript is elided. Also, the
    following properties hold:
    \begin{align*}
        \mathrm{id}\circ f = f = f\circ\mathrm{id} & &
        (f \circ g) \circ h = f \circ (g \circ h)
    \end{align*}
    for arbitrary morphisms $f,g,h$ with matching ends.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Terminal object]
Terminal object of category $\mathcal{C}$ is an object
$\top\in\mathrm{Ob}_\mathcal{C}$ such that for each
$A\in\mathrm{Ob}_\mathcal{C}$ there's a \textbf{unique}
morphism $!_A:A\to\top$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Presheaf]
A presheaf $P$ on a category $\mathcal{C}$ (written as
$P:\mathcal{C}^\mathrm{op}\to\mathrm{Set}$) is a mapping
which takes objects of $\mathcal{C}$ to sets, and
morphisms of $\mathcal{C}$ to functions between sets, so
that if $f:A\to_\mathcal{C} B$, then $P(f)$ is a
function from $P(B)$ to $P(A)$. Also, the following
properties hold:
\begin{align*}
    P(\mathrm{id}_A)=\mathrm{id}_{P(A)} & &
    P(g\circ f)=P(f)\circ P(g)
\end{align*}
Where $\mathrm{id}_{P(A)}$ is an identity function of a
set $P(A)$ and $P(f)\circ P(g)$ is a composition of
functions $P(f)$ and $P(g)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Category of elements]
Given a presheaf $P:\mathcal{C}^\mathrm{op}\to
\mathrm{Set}$, a category of elements
$\int_\mathcal{C}P$ is a category where objects are
pairs $(A, x)$ such that $A\in\mathrm{Ob}_\mathcal{C}$
and $x\in P(A)$, and morphisms from $(A, x)$ to $(B, y)$
are morphisms $f:A\to_\mathcal{C} B$ such that
$P(f)(y)=x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Category with families]\label{cwfdef}
A category with families is the following datum:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A \textit{category of contexts} $\mathcal{C}$
        which is a category with a terminal object;
    \item A \textit{presheaf of types} $\mathrm{Ty}:
        \mathcal{C}^\mathrm{op}\to\mathrm{Set}$. Given a
        morphism $\gamma: \Gamma\to_\mathcal{C}\Delta$
        and a \textit{type} $T\in\mathrm{Ty}(\Delta)$,
        element $\mathrm{Ty}(\gamma)(T)\in
        \mathrm{Ty}(\Gamma)$ is usually written as
        $T[\gamma]$ for brevity.
    \item A \textit{presheaf of terms} $\mathrm{Tm}:
        \left(\int_\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Ty}\right
        )^\mathrm{op}\to\mathrm{Set}$. Same as with
        types, $\mathrm{Tm}(\gamma)(t)\in\mathrm{Tm}
        (\Gamma, T)$ is usually written as $t[\gamma]$
        for brevity.
    \item For each $\Gamma\in\mathrm{Ob}_\mathcal{C}$
        and $T\in\mathrm{Ty}(\Gamma)$, there is an
        \textit{extended context}
        $\Gamma.T\in\mathrm{Ob}_\mathcal{C}$ together
        with a \textit{weakening morphism}
        $p:\Gamma.T\to\Gamma$ and \textit{variable
        element} $q:\mathrm{Tm}(\Gamma.T, T[p])$. In
        addition, for all $\Delta$,
        $\gamma:\Delta\to\Gamma$ and
        $t\in\mathrm{Tm}(\Delta, T[\gamma])$, there is a
        unique \textit{extended substitution}
        $\langle\gamma,t\rangle:\Delta\to\Gamma.T$ such
        that the following holds:
        \begin{align*}
            p\circ\langle\gamma,t\rangle=\gamma & &
            q[\langle \gamma, t \rangle] = t
        \end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A definition of a Cwf (def. \ref{cwfdef}) clearly
captures the bare-bones structure of a dependently
typed theory: objects of $\mathcal{C}$ represent
contexts, while morphisms represent substitutions
mapping one context to another. Substitution also acts
both on types and terms; given a correct type $T$ in
context $\Gamma$, one can extend a context to gain new
context $\Gamma,x:T$ in which there's a well-typed term
$x:T$; there's a way to obtain substitutions into an
extended context by providing a term suitable for $x$.

This base definition can be readily extended to
incorporate type (and term) formers of any system. A CwF
for MLTT-R, then, has to include $\mathrm{Ty}$- and
$\mathrm{Tm}$- formers for analogues of universes,
$\Pi$- and $\Sigma$- types, natural numbers and identity
types. Interpretation for $\Box_\ell$ is not needed
since we succesfully prevented boxed types from
appearing inside boxed terms via rigid \faLock-ing
discipline\footnote{That's also the reason we dubbed
this system \textit{almost} reflective, since its
internal reinterpretation doesn't cover modalities and
reinterpretation itself.}. Thus, contexts which need to
be interpreted are constructed via vanilla context
extensions from empty context, and a usual CwF is
enough for our purposes. Since actual internalized
definition of our CwF largely repeats typing and
computation rules from figures
\ref{mltt-r-wf}-\ref{mltt-r-typing} and
\ref{mltt-r-typcomp}-\ref{mltt-r-comp} in internal
language, we do not provide it here; to dive into
details, you can refer to our supplementary materials.

Finally, on to computation rules as given in figures
\ref{mltt-r-typcomp}-\ref{mltt-r-comp} (full typal
computation relation is spelled as
$\Gamma\vdash_\ell T\leadsto T'$, while term computation
is spelled as $\Gamma\vdash_\ell t\leadsto t'
\Leftrightarrow T$; when type is not important and
context or level bound is simply propagated, they are
omitted for brevity). Thanks to internalized definition
of a CwF, we know how $\mathcal{M}$ computes; most of
the remaining computation rules for types and terms are
standard MLTT computation rules for bidirectional
typing (without $\eta$-equality for now). Importantly
enough, we \textbf{do not compute inside locked terms
and inside boxed types} in order to not stumble into a
variant of our no-go theorem \ref{no-go} again (modal
variant is spelled out in discussion, see section
\ref{global-elements}).

Also we need to give the rules for computing
reinterpretation; as it turns out,
\textit{reinterpreting terms as-is} allows to define
reinterpretation not as a redex, but rather as a couple
of total functions on types and terms, just as it was in
a non-dependently-typed case (section \ref{HM-R}). To be
precise, we need two mutually recursive functions for
terms: $\llbracket t\rrbracket^\Gamma_{\Leftarrow T}
\mathtt{m}$ computes on checkable terms, while
$\llbracket t\rrbracket^\Gamma_{\Rightarrow}\mathtt{m}$
computes on synthesizing terms and outputs an inferred
type; both compute elements of corresponding
$\mathrm{Tm}(\Gamma,\_)$-s from a CwF $\mathtt{m}$.
$T^\Gamma_\mathtt{m}$ computes an element of
$\mathrm{Ty}(\Gamma)$. Another auxiliary function is
required to compute chain of equalities to handle
conversion rule; this function has two subtle points:

\begin{itemize}
    \item At this point, we don't know if system is
        strongly normalizing yet, therefore we
        potentially might encounter an infinite chain of
        equalities.
    \item In systems where equality is proof-relevant
        (and we want to be able to develop in that
        direction), different reduction chains might
        lead to different equality witnesses, so such a
        function is potentially not well-defined.
\end{itemize}

Our \textit{hacky} solution is the following: let us
fix a certain evaluation strategy (for example, strict
evaluation) and employ it both during bidirectional
type checking and when running a witness extraction
function. If we run a witness extraction function on a
term that was successfully typed by typing algorithm,
this typing algorithm finished running, and then
witness extraction function would terminate, too. Since
evaluation strategy is fixed, witness extraction
function would always produce the same output on the
same input and is therefore well-defined. Once again,
since interpretation functions directly translate
bidirectional typing rules on terms into applications
of model constructions, definition of interpretation
functions is moved to supplementary materials.

\subsection{Metatheoretical properties of MLTT-R}
\label{MLTT-R-properties}

Once again, we only try to make sure of the most
essential properties: that computations are confluent,
that types during computations are preserved, all
computations terminate and that resulting type system
is a conservative extension of MLTT.

\begin{theorem}[Church-Rosser theorem for MLTT-R]
    Reflexive transitive closure of $(\leadsto)$ has
    Church-Rosser property.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Classic proof via parallel reduction relation
    \cite{CRproof}. Since interpretation computes as a
    recursive functions on open terms, it commutes with
    substitution, which is crucial for confluence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Type preservation for MLTT-R]
    If $\Gamma\vdash t\Leftarrow T$ and $t\leadsto t'$,
    then $\Gamma\vdash t'\Leftarrow T$; analogously for
    $\Gamma\vdash t\Rightarrow T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    By structural induction on terms and $(\leadsto)$.
    Note that, in bidirectional system, one has to take
    care to structure rules in such a way that
    substitutions substitute terms that are inferred, so
    that inferrable terms stay inferrable and checkable
    terms stay checkable.
    Analogous to the proof of type preservation for HM-R
    (section \ref{HM-R-properties}), a vital lemma
    holds:
    \[  \dfrac{
            \Gamma.{}_\ell\text{\faLock},\Delta
                \vdash_\kappa t\Leftarrow T
            \quad\Gamma\vdash_\ell s\Leftarrow
                \mathcal{M}^{\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_\kappa}
        }{\Gamma\vdash_\ell\llbracket t\rrbracket_{
            \Leftarrow T}\Rightarrow
            s.\mathrm{Tm}(s\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket,
                T_s^\Delta)}
    \]
    Along with similar lemmas for $\llbracket\_
    \rrbracket_\Rightarrow$ and $\__s^\Delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Identity model]
    For each $\ell$, there is an identity model
    $I_\ell:\mathcal{M}_{0,\ldots,\ell}$ with a property
    that $\llbracket [t]_\text{\faLock} \rrbracket
    I_\ell \equiv t$ for each closed $t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Take a universe at appropriate level as type of
    contexts; $\mathrm{Ty}_\ell(\Gamma)=
    \Gamma\to\mathcal{U}_\ell$;
    $\mathrm{Tm}_\ell(\Gamma, T)=(\gamma:\Gamma)\to
    T\,\gamma$. Define all of the remaining term
    interpretations to reinterpret to the same terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Strong normalization for MLTT-R]
\label{mltt-r-sn}
    Closed terms typable in MLTT-R are strongly
    normalizing with respect to $(\leadsto)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Any proof of normalization for MLTT suffices. For
    techniques via Tait's logical relations (as was done
    for HM-R in section \ref{HM-R-properties}), take
    $t\in\mathcal{R}_{\Box_\kappa T}$ to mean
    ``interpretation of $t$ is in $\mathcal{R}$ for
    interpretation of $T$ for all internal models in
    $\mathcal{R}_\mathcal{M}$''. For techniques via
    normalization-by-evaluation as in \cite{ABEL200717},
    take value of such $t$ to be its interpretation
    function; to quote value back into a term, run this
    function with an identity model.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conservativity over MLTT]
    For MLTT context $\Gamma$, MLTT type $T$ and MLTT-R
    term $t$, if $\Gamma\vdash t\Leftarrow T$, then
    $\Gamma\vdash_\mathrm{MLTT}\mathrm{nf}(t)\Leftarrow
    T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Thanks to type preservation, normal form
    $\mathrm{nf}(t)$ is typable in MLTT-R. Since
    $\Gamma$ does not contain neither $\Box$-es nor
    \faLock-s, any subterm of type $\Box_\kappa T$
    has form $[\_]_\text{\faLock}$. Since $T$ is an MLTT
    type, every such locking is wrapped either in a
    $[\_]_\text{\faUnlock}$ or in a $\llbracket\_
    \rrbracket$. Second case is a redex and is
    impossible in a normal form; $[\_]_\text{\faUnlock}$
    is possible only in a locked context, so that means
    that there's another $[\_]_\text{\faLock}$ above it.
    Therefore $\mathrm{nf}(t)$ has no subterms of type
    $\Box_\kappa T$; then it has no unlocking and
    interpretation subterms and is an MLTT term and is
    typable in bidirectional MLTT (it is obtained from
    MLTT-R by getting rid of rules involving modalities,
    locking and reinterpretation).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Useful reinterpretations inside MLTT-R}
\label{MLTT-R-applications}

Here we list some of the language features which
reinterpretation enables without any additional language
support.

\subsubsection{Crude, but effective stratification}
\label{MLTT-R-displacement}

Due to McBride \cite{McBride:Crude2011,
McBride:Crude2012}, a technique of crude, but effective
stratification works as follows: instead of providing
complete support for level polymorphism, let's consider
a \textit{displacement operator} which, given a term $t$
using universes $U_i,U_j,U_k,\ldots$, produces a term
which is equal to $t$ except for universes which are
replaced with $U_{i+1},U_{j+1},U_{k+1},\ldots$. The
displaced term is also well-typed, however it is defined
<<one level higher>>. While not directly definable as a
single operator inside MLTT-R, for each $\kappa,\ell$,
one can define displacement operators
$\uparrow_\ell^\kappa:\mathcal{M}_{\kappa,
\kappa+1,\ldots,\kappa+\ell}$ that shifts a boxed
definition which uses universes up to $\mathcal{U}_\ell$
by $\kappa$ levels up. In this model, category of
contexts basic presheaves
are shifted by $\kappa$ in comparison to an identity
model; all other term interpretations are identical to
the ones given in an identity model.

Full definition of $\uparrow_\ell^\kappa$ is given in
supplementary materials.

\subsubsection{Internal external parametricity}
\label{MLTT-R-parametricity}

Demonstrated by Reynolds \cite{reynolds1983types},
parametricity is a property of a type system that, in
simple words, <<polymorphic function cannot
meaningfully depend on generic inputs>>. Thus any
polymorphic function of type $\sigma$ satisfies a
certain <<free theorem>> $\llbracket \sigma
\rrbracket$ defined via relational interpretation of a
type.

In recent works \cite{Bernardy:Parametricity2010,
Altenkirch:Parametricity2024}, a streamlined definition
is given for parametricity interpretation of
dependently-typed systems dubbed <<external
parametricity>>. Once again, this relational
interpretation is definable in our system
\textit{internally} with the help of a relational model
$\mathrm{Para}_\ell:\mathcal{M}_{1,\ldots,\ell+1}$. The
shift by one level happens because type
$T:\mathcal{U}_\ell$ in such a model is interpreted by
a triple $(T_1\,T_2:\mathcal{U}_\ell)\times
(T_1\to T_2\to\mathcal{U}_\ell)$.
Since interpreted functions do not depend on local
context, we sidestep issues with relational context
highlighted by Altenkirch and others
\cite{Altenkirch:Parametricity2024}, thus result of the
interpretation is a proper proof of a free theorem.

Full definition of $\mathrm{Para}_\ell$ is given in
supplementary materials.

\subsubsection{Synthetic mathematics}
\label{MLTT-R-synthetic}

As thoroughly discussed in a recent paper by Nawrocki
and others \cite{Nawrocki:Lean2026}, thanks to MLTT
being an internal language of certain
$(\infty,1)$-categories, we can do \textit{synthetic
mathematics} inside it: prove a theorem inside MLTT
about types; interpret it in a model of MLTT and yield
a proof about objects in that model. It is easy to see
that reinterpretation operator we have in MLTT-R is
sufficient to do synthetic mathematics in it and
immediately apply results interpreted in a suitable
model.
